Surviving Maths 1 & 2: Why Proofs Matter for Machine Learning

Connecting undergraduate linear algebra to gradient descent and loss surfaces

First-term IIT Madras BS learners frequently encounter an abrupt cultural shock in Mathematics 1 and 2. High school mathematics is largely procedural: compute this integral, solve for x, invert this 3x3 matrix. But when faced with formal proof verification, linear independence definitions, and nullspace characterizations, rote methods collapse.

The Bridge from Axioms to High-Dimensional Spaces

Why do professors insist on proving that eigenvectors of symmetric matrices corresponding to distinct eigenvalues are orthogonal? Because in machine learning, Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and singular value decomposition depend fundamentally on this exact geometric guarantee.

“When you train an 8-billion parameter model, you are navigating an 8-billion dimensional non-convex manifold. Intuition without linear algebraic rigor leads straight into numerical instability.”

Weekly Study Protocol for Mathematics 1 & 2

To score consistently above 85% in proctored quizzes:

1. Do not skip weekly practice assignments: Attempt proofs without viewing solution keys for at least 45 minutes.
2. Translate algebra into geometry: Sketch 2D and 3D transformations for every theorem involving rank, kernel, and matrix multiplication.
3. Form a proof pod: Trade problem explanations with peers in the IITian Tattva community discord.
